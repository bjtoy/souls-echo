## 1. Purpose and Approval Boundary

This policy defines minimum privacy and security requirements for future Gate 1
screening, recruitment, consent, interviews, notes, recordings, analysis and
reporting. It does not authorise recruitment, screening, contact, interviews or
data collection.

This document cannot be used operationally until:

- real project contact details are approved;
- provisional retention rules are approved for every listed data category;
- data locations and access roles are approved;
- document versions and effective dates are set;
- all participant-facing placeholders are removed;
- proposed third-party tools are assessed; and
- all required governance and research materials are approved.

No survey, recruitment, recording, transcription, cloud-storage, payment or other
external platform is approved by this document.

## 2. Data Categories

### Identifiable Data

Information that directly identifies a person or readily enables identification,
including names, contact details, voice recordings, consent signatures,
scheduling correspondence, exact locations and combinations of distinctive
personal details.

### Pseudonymised Data

Research material linked to a participant identifier while a separate mapping
can reconnect that identifier to a person. Pseudonymised information remains
personal information and must not be described as anonymous.

### De-Identified Research Data

Material from which direct identifiers and unnecessary identifying context have
been removed. Removing a name does not make material anonymous when content,
context or other records could still reasonably identify the participant.

### Aggregated Findings

Summaries combining information from multiple participants so individual
responses are not reasonably distinguishable. Aggregation does not make a unique
quotation safe when its wording or context could identify its source.

## 3. Data Minimisation

Collect only information necessary for eligibility, sample balance, scheduling,
informed consent, approved research questions, incentives where separately
approved, withdrawal, research-quality controls or established obligations.

Do not collect by default:

- exact residential addresses;
- exact dates of birth;
- partner, family or colleague names;
- contact lists;
- identity documents;
- detailed traumatic experiences;
- medical or legal histories;
- precise location; or
- unrelated demographic information.

Free-text responses must be reviewed for unnecessary personal details.

## 4. Early Diagnostic Review Minimisation

The first five problem interviews form an **Early Diagnostic Review**, not
validation. Screening for those interviews must use a reduced field set limited
to:

- adult eligibility;
- voluntary participation;
- broad separation or relationship context;
- likely recipient or purchaser perspective;
- wearable use or scepticism;
- broad Australian location;
- recruitment source; and
- one approved scheduling contact collected after selection.

Do not collect optional accessibility details, recording preference, detailed
maintenance tolerance or other non-essential screening information during this
review unless a later approved research reason requires it. Recording consent
belongs at the interview-consent stage, not initial selection.

Positive evidence from five interviews cannot validate the problem, product,
market or Gate 1. Strong negative evidence may justify pausing expenditure and
reviewing the method.

## 5. Participant Identifiers

Use sequential, non-semantic identifiers such as `P001`, `P002` and `P003`.
Identifiers must not encode relationship type, gender, age, location,
accessibility information, recruitment source, interview outcome or another
personal characteristic.

The participant-ID mapping is identifiable data and must be stored separately
with more restricted access than research notes.

## 6. Separation of Records

Maintain separate logical records for:

1. contact and scheduling information;
2. participant-ID mapping;
3. consent records;
4. screening responses;
5. recordings;
6. pseudonymised interview notes;
7. recruitment-source records;
8. incentives, if separately approved; and
9. de-identified analysis and aggregated findings.

Research notes should use only the participant identifier where practical.

## 7. Screening Responses

The full screener may be used only for a later approved wider Gate 1 recruitment
stage. The reduced Early Diagnostic Review field set applies to the first five
interviews.

Do not retain unnecessary responses from people who are ineligible or not
selected. Recruitment-source information may be retained in de-identified or
aggregated form for bias analysis.

## 8. Consent Records

Consent records must preserve:

- the participant's choices;
- document versions supplied;
- the activity covered;
- consent date;
- recording choice;
- quotation choice; and
- follow-up-contact choice.

Consent for one activity must not be reused for another unspecified activity.
Consent records containing identifying information must be protected as
identifiable data.

## 9. Recordings and Transcription

Recording requires a separate affirmative choice. A participant may decline
recording where the approved research method can reasonably proceed using notes.

Before recording, approve and disclose:

- recording format;
- purpose;
- storage location;
- permitted access;
- transcription method, if any;
- verification process; and
- deletion milestone.

Do not automatically upload recordings to an external service. Automated
transcription and other third-party tools must not receive participant information
until separately assessed. De-identified notes should become the primary research
record where practical.

## 10. Interview Notes

Notes must distinguish:

- participant statements;
- observed behaviour;
- researcher interpretation;
- uncertainty; and
- follow-up questions.

Do not record unnecessary names or distinctive personal details. Remove such
details during prompt de-identification.

## 11. Recruitment-Source Records

Record broad recruitment source so convenience and founder-network bias can be
assessed. Suitable categories include founder-connected, referral, public
recruitment, community group, professional or interest group, and recruitment
service. Do not expose unnecessary group membership in findings.

## 12. Access Control

Access must be limited by role and operational need. Before collection, document:

- who may access contact and scheduling data;
- who may access consent records;
- who may access recordings;
- who may access pseudonymised notes;
- who may access the participant-ID mapping; and
- who may approve quotation or external sharing.

Avoid shared credentials and remove access when no longer required. Exceptional
administrative access must not become routine access.

## 13. Storage

### Local Storage

Any approved local storage must use an access-controlled device, device
encryption where available, current operating-system security, non-shared user
accounts and automatic locking. Avoid uncontrolled removable media.

### Cloud Storage

No cloud provider is approved. Any later proposal requires privacy and security
assessment before participant information is uploaded.

### Git Prohibition

Participant personal information must never be committed to Git. This includes:

- contact and scheduling details;
- consent signatures;
- participant-ID mappings;
- recordings;
- raw screener exports;
- identifiable or unreviewed notes;
- payment details; and
- unreviewed free-text responses.

Repository artefacts may contain approved methodology, templates, appropriately
de-identified analysis or aggregated findings only after review.

## 14. Third-Party Tools

Any external tool receiving participant information requires a separate privacy
and security assessment covering purpose, data received, access, storage
location, retention, deletion, provider reuse or model training, subprocessors,
security, contractual terms, cross-border processing and less invasive options.

No recruitment, survey, recording, transcription, cloud-storage, payment or
research platform is approved in this policy.

## 15. Backups

Backup treatment must be approved before collection. Document whether each data
category is backed up, backup location, access, encryption, retention, deletion
limitations and how withdrawal interacts with backup expiry.

Do not promise immediate deletion from backups if the approved process cannot
provide it. Explain practical limitations truthfully.

## 16. Provisional Gate 1 Retention Schedule

A completed provisional schedule must be approved before recruitment. These
rules are operational proposals, not claims of legally required retention.
Milestone-based rules are preferred where practical.

| Data category | Approved provisional rule required before collection |
|---|---|
| Unselected screener responses | [For example: delete after selection and the associated eligibility review are complete; retain only de-identified recruitment-source counts where required for bias analysis.] |
| Scheduling and contact details | [For example: delete after the relevant session, payment, withdrawal and approved follow-up purposes are complete.] |
| Participant-ID mapping | [For example: retain only until the Gate 1 decision and applicable withdrawal period are complete, then delete unless a separately assessed requirement applies.] |
| Consent records | [Approve a purpose-based milestone after privacy, legal, university and operational requirements are assessed.] |
| Recordings | [For example: delete after verified notes are completed and recording-based quality review is no longer required.] |
| Pseudonymised notes | [For example: retain through the Gate 1 decision, contradiction review and applicable withdrawal period, then de-identify further or delete under an approved rule.] |
| Follow-up contact permission | [For example: retain only while the specific approved follow-up purpose remains operationally necessary or until withdrawn.] |
| Aggregated or de-identified findings | [Approve a project-purpose rule that considers residual re-identification risk and continuing research value.] |

Before recruitment, replace every placeholder with a clear rule, responsible role,
trigger and deletion action. Do not state that a period is legally required unless
that has been established.

A properly assessed legal, accounting, university or professional requirement
may override a provisional rule. Record the source and effect of any override.

## 17. Withdrawal

Before collection, specify the milestone until which identifiable or
pseudonymised material can practicably be located and withdrawn. A withdrawal
request should require only enough information to locate the record.

After information has been fully de-identified, aggregated or incorporated into
analysis, individual removal may no longer be technically reliable. This
limitation must be disclosed truthfully.

Record the request, scope, action, completion and any backup or aggregation
limitation without reproducing the withdrawn information unnecessarily.

## 18. Deletion

Use approved deletion methods appropriate to the storage medium. Deletion should
cover active copies, exports, temporary files, working copies and third-party
services where applicable. A deletion record must not reproduce the deleted
sensitive information.

## 19. Quotations and External Sharing

Use quotations only with the applicable separate permission. Remove names and
unnecessary identifying context, assess distinctive wording and prefer paraphrase
or aggregation where re-identification risk remains.

Do not publish raw transcripts. Participant-level external sharing requires
separate justification, appropriate consent, privacy review and an approved
transfer process.

## 20. Research Summaries

Summaries must distinguish evidence from interpretation, retain contradictory
evidence, avoid treating qualitative counts as population prevalence, disclose
sample and recruitment limitations and use de-identified or aggregated material.

## 21. Security Incidents and Accidental Disclosure

Potential incidents include lost devices, unauthorised access, information sent
to the wrong person, participant data committed to Git, unapproved cloud upload,
exposed recordings or compromised third-party services.

On discovery:

1. stop further disclosure where possible;
2. restrict or revoke access;
3. preserve only necessary evidence without spreading participant data;
4. identify affected records and likely harm;
5. seek appropriate privacy, legal or security advice;
6. notify affected people or authorities where required or appropriate;
7. correct the cause;
8. record the incident without duplicating exposed data; and
9. reassess whether research should pause.

Do not conceal an incident to protect the project.

## 22. Researcher Conduct

Researchers must not argue with participants, persuade them to like the concept,
disclose another participant's responses, pressure continued participation,
diagnose relationship problems, offer counselling, provide legal or medical
advice, or promise confidentiality beyond what the project can provide.

Stop or redirect questioning if distress becomes apparent. Record contradictory
evidence faithfully and separate observation from interpretation.

## 23. University and Specialist Review

Gate 1 is currently product and customer discovery and is not represented as
university-approved research. If an activity becomes connected to formal academic
research, publication, university resources, university recruitment or assessment
involving human participants, applicable university requirements must be checked
before that activity.

Qualified specialist review may be required for technology-facilitated abuse,
coercive-control scenarios, distress processes or other risks beyond general
customer-research expertise.

## 24. Contact Privacy

When a research contact is approved, prefer a dedicated project contact channel
over unnecessary personal contact details. No address or contact method is
approved or invented in this document.

## 25. Pre-Collection Approval

Before collection begins, approve:

- final document versions and effective dates;
- real dedicated project contact and withdrawal channels;
- provisional retention rules for every category;
- data locations and access roles;
- backup treatment;
- recruitment copy and interview guide;
- the Early Diagnostic Review procedure;
- any incentive and payment process;
- every external tool receiving participant information; and
- applicable legal, privacy, specialist or university requirements.
