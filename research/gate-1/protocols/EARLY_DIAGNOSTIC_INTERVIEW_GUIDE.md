## 1. Purpose

The Early Diagnostic Review consists of five neutral problem interviews conducted
before concept exposure to:

- test the interview method;
- detect leading, confusing or misunderstood questions;
- identify strong negative evidence;
- identify recruitment problems;
- identify unexpected themes; and
- decide whether wider Gate 1 research should continue unchanged, be redesigned, pause or receive a stop/pivot review.

Five interviews cannot validate Soul's Echo, establish market prevalence, prove
willingness to pay, produce Gate 1 PASS or justify Gate 2 progression.

## 2. Critical No-Concept-Exposure Rule

During these five interviews, the interviewer must not explain or reveal:

- the Soul's Echo Band;
- remote physical interaction;
- haptic output;
- light output;
- phone-independent local inhibit;
- the Companion App;
- the proposed technical system;
- proposed consent-operation designs; or
- intended product features.

Do not ask whether the participant would buy Soul's Echo, whether they like the
idea or which proposed feature they prefer. Do not conduct the later consent,
pause, block, disconnect, permission or local-inhibit comprehension test.

If a participant independently suggests a similar idea, ask about the underlying
need and their existing behaviour without confirming, comparing or describing the
Soul's Echo solution. Record spontaneous suggestions faithfully.

If a participant asks what Soul's Echo intends to build, use the approved neutral
boundary response:

> This first discussion is intended to understand existing experiences before we
> introduce any proposed solution. I do not want the concept to influence your
> answers. A later, separately approved research stage may discuss concepts.

## 3. Operational Preconditions

Do not conduct an interview until:

- recruitment and screening are separately authorised;
- approved Participant Information has been supplied;
- consent has been recorded through the approved process;
- a real dedicated contact and withdrawal channel is available;
- data location, access, retention, backup and deletion rules are approved;
- all operational placeholders are resolved;
- the notes-only process is approved;
- any recording process and technology are separately approved; and
- required privacy, specialist or university reviews are complete.

No recording, transcription, scheduling, storage or external research technology
is selected by this guide.

## 4. Proposed Session Outline

**Proposed duration:** [APPROVED DURATION REQUIRED]

Indicative allocation, subject to pilot review rather than participant pressure:

1. opening and consent confirmation;
2. recent separation experience;
3. existing communication;
4. unmet or fully met moments;
5. recipient perspective and ordinary boundaries;
6. wearable behaviour and maintenance;
7. alternatives and workarounds; and

Do not rush a participant to complete every topic. A skipped question is not a

Before substantive questions:

- [ ] confirm the approved Participant Information was supplied;
- [ ] confirm consent choices using the approved process;
- [ ] confirm note-taking permission;
- [ ] confirm recording choice only if recording has separately been operationally approved;
- [ ] remind the participant that participation is voluntary;
- [ ] remind them that any question may be skipped;
- [ ] remind them that they may take a break or stop at any time;
- [ ] explain that disagreement and negative experiences are useful;
- [ ] explain that it is legitimate to say nothing was missing;
- [ ] ask them not to disclose information they do not wish to share; and
- [ ] explain that the session is not counselling, medical, legal, relationship or crisis support.

### Proposed Opening

> Thank you for considering this discussion. We are exploring how adults
> communicate during periods of physical separation. We are interested in what
> actually happens, including situations where existing communication works
> completely well.
>
> There are no correct answers. Negative feedback and disagreement are useful.
> You may skip any question, ask to change topic, take a break or stop at any
> time. Please do not share anything you are uncomfortable discussing.
>
> This first interview will not present or test a product concept. I may ask for
> recent examples so that I understand your experience rather than guessing.

Do not improvise promises about confidentiality, withdrawal, recording, payment
or future products beyond approved participant materials.

## 6. Recent Separation Experience

Begin with a recent, real example.

### Core Questions

1. Think about the most recent time you were physically apart from someone important to you in a way that affected how you communicated. What was happening?
2. Approximately when was that?
3. What made the separation relevant to communication, if anything?
4. How long did the situation last?
5. What communication, if any, took place?
6. What prompted the first communication?
7. What did you actually do?
8. What happened afterwards?

### Neutral Probes

- Can you walk me through that moment?
- What happened next?
- Who initiated the communication, if anyone?
- What made you choose that method?
- Was that typical or unusual?
- Is there a more recent example?

Do not ask for names, exact locations, employer details or unnecessary personal
context.

## 7. Existing Communication Behaviour

Explore only methods the participant uses or recognises. Do not imply inadequacy.

### Core Questions

1. What methods do you ordinarily use when you are apart?
2. Which do you use most often, and in what situations?
3. How do you decide between messaging, calls or another method?
4. Are voice messages, video calls, social media or wearables relevant to you?
5. What works particularly well?
6. What tends not to fit, if anything?

### Behaviour Probes

- Tell me about the last time you used that method.
- What did you do rather than what you intended to do?
- How did the other person respond, if they did?
- What made that outcome satisfactory or unsatisfactory?

Do not turn a neutral inconvenience into an unmet emotional problem.

## 8. Unmet or Fully Met Moments

Only explore unmet moments after ordinary behaviour is understood.

### Core Questions

1. In the example you described, was anything missing from the available communication?
2. If nothing was missing, what made the existing approach sufficient?
3. If something was missing, what happened?
4. What were you trying to communicate or experience?
5. What did you do instead?
6. Did you decide to do nothing? What happened then?
7. How important was the situation at the time?
8. Have similar situations happened again?
9. Can you describe the most recent similar example?

The answer **nothing was missing** is legitimate evidence. Do not challenge it,
suggest alternatives or search for a problem until the participant agrees one
exists.

### Evidence-of-Sufficiency Probes

- What made existing communication enough?
- Was there any reason to want another method?
- Would adding another method have improved anything, complicated it or made no difference?

Do not interpret desire for convenience automatically as willingness to adopt a
dedicated wearable.

## 9. Recipient Perspective

Explore occasions when the participant primarily received rather than initiated
communication.

### Core Questions

1. Tell me about a recent time someone contacted you while you were apart.
2. What made the contact welcome, neutral or unwelcome?
3. Did the timing matter?
4. Did it interrupt anything?
5. Did you feel expected to respond immediately?
6. What did you do?
7. What happened if you did not respond?
8. Did silence create pressure or misunderstanding?
9. How did that compare with times when you initiated contact?

Do not introduce proposed protective controls or ask participants to design them.

## 10. Ordinary Boundaries

Use non-trauma-seeking questions.

### Core Questions

1. Are there ordinary times when you prefer to be unavailable?
2. How do people close to you usually understand that?
3. What happens when someone contacts you repeatedly?
4. When does repetition feel welcome, distracting or unwanted?
5. What expectations exist around responding?
6. Should another person know why you did not respond? Why or why not?
7. What information, if any, should remain private?

Do not ask participants to disclose abuse, traumatic histories or reasons for
ending relationships. If sensitive disclosure arises voluntarily, acknowledge it
without probing merely because it is interesting. Offer to pause, redirect or
stop.

## 11. Wearable, Jewellery and Device Behaviour

Do not describe the proposed Band.

### Core Questions

1. Do you currently wear jewellery, a watch or another wearable regularly?
2. What makes you continue wearing it?
3. Have you stopped using or regularly wearing one? What changed?
4. How does comfort affect whether you keep using it?
5. How does appearance affect use?
6. What charging or maintenance do you tolerate now?
7. Tell me about the last time charging or maintenance affected use.
8. How do privacy concerns affect wearable use, if at all?
9. What makes a wearable worth the burden of wearing and maintaining it?

### Sceptic Probes

- What do you dislike or distrust about wearables?
- Have you deliberately avoided one? What informed that decision?
- What would not be worth adding another device for?

Do not promise a battery duration or infer that design can remove every burden.

## 12. Alternatives and Workarounds

### Core Questions

1. When ordinary communication does not fit the situation, what do you currently do?
2. Can you describe the last time you used that workaround?
3. Do you delay communication, use a symbol or routine, rely on another device, ask someone else, or do nothing?
4. What works well about that approach?
5. What does not work well, if anything?
6. Have you paid for or adopted anything intended to help? What happened?

Look for existing behaviour. Do not convert speculative feature requests into
evidence of demand.

## 13. Closing and Method Feedback

The first five interviews test the research method as well as assumptions.

### Core Questions

1. Is there anything important about staying connected during separation that I failed to ask?
2. Which questions were confusing or unclear?
3. Did any question feel uncomfortable or ask for information you did not want to share?
4. Which questions felt repetitive or difficult to answer?
5. Did any question appear to push you towards a particular answer?
6. Is there anything you believe I misunderstood?
7. Is there anything you would remove or change about this interview?

### Closing Script

> Thank you. This interview is part of an early review of the research method. It
> does not validate a product or guarantee that Soul's Echo will proceed. Please
> use the approved project contact if you later have a question or wish to make a
> withdrawal request within the explained process.

**Dedicated project contact:** [APPROVED CONTACT METHOD REQUIRED]

Do not reveal the product concept after the interview unless a later approved
process explicitly permits debriefing without contaminating possible future
research participation.

## 14. Interviewer Conduct Rules

- Do not sell, explain or defend Soul's Echo.
- Do not reveal the intended solution.
- Do not complete a participant's sentence.
- Do not convert neutral statements into problems.
- Do not treat emotional language as proof of demand.
- Ask for examples.
- Ask “what happened?” before “what would you want?”.
- Use silence rather than suggesting answers.
- Distinguish participant statements from interpretation.
- Record contradictory evidence.
- Do not pressure a participant to identify an unmet need.
- Do not interpret politeness as interest.
- Do not seek traumatic disclosure.
- Stop or redirect if distress becomes apparent.
- Do not give counselling, medical, legal, relationship or crisis advice.
- Do not disclose another participant's responses.
- Do not diagnose the participant or their relationships.
- Do not imply confidentiality beyond approved technical and operational controls.
- Do not add unapproved questions merely because a disclosure is interesting.

## 15. Neutral Probe Library

Permitted probes include:

- Tell me more about what happened.
- Can you give me a recent example?
- What happened next?
- What did you do?
- How did you decide that?
- What made that important or unimportant?
- Was that typical?
- What makes you say that?
- What worked well?
- Was anything missing?
- It is completely acceptable if nothing was missing.

Avoid:

- Wouldn't it be better if...?
- Did that make you feel disconnected?
- Would you like a more intimate way to connect?
- Would you buy something that solved that?
- How much would you pay?
- Did you wish you could physically feel their presence?
- Would a wearable help?

## 16. Diagnostic Hypothesis Links

The interviews may provide early evidence relevant to:

- `HYP-001` — whether a recurring unmet problem appears spontaneously;
- `HYP-003` — differences between sender and recipient experiences;
- `HYP-004` — actual wearable acceptance and abandonment behaviour;
- `HYP-005` — ordinary intrusion, timing and response-pressure concerns; and
- `HYP-013` — actual charging and maintenance burden.

No hypothesis may be labelled validated or falsified solely from five
interviews. Strong negative evidence may still justify pausing or escalating.

## 18. Per-Interview Protocol Review

After each session, review without changing the guide mid-wave unless safety or a
serious method flaw requires immediate action:

- Were any questions leading?
- Were any questions misunderstood?
- Could the participant provide recent examples?
- Was “nothing missing” accepted without pressure?
- Did the interviewer reveal or imply the solution?
- Did sensitive disclosure arise, and was it handled without probing?
- Was evidence separated from interpretation?
- Did recruitment provide the intended perspective variation?
- Is immediate pause or governance escalation required?

Document material deviations. Do not silently repair the record after learning a
participant's response.

## 19. Post-Five-Interview Diagnostic Review

Permitted recommendations are:

- **CONTINUE UNCHANGED**
- **REVISE METHOD**
- **PAUSE RESEARCH**
- **ESCALATE TO STOP/PIVOT REVIEW**

Five interviews cannot produce:

- Gate 1 PASS;
- product validation;
- market validation;
- statistically meaningful prevalence;
- proof of willingness to pay; or
- approval to enter Gate 2.

Strong negative evidence may justify stopping expenditure, redesigning the
remaining Gate 1 method or escalating earlier than planned.

### Review Questions

1. Were questions leading, confusing, repetitive or uncomfortable?
2. Did participants understand what was being asked?
3. Did recruitment provide useful variation in recipient/purchaser perspective, context, wearable use, location and source?
4. Were recent behavioural examples obtainable?
5. Did the hypothesised problem appear spontaneously, or only after prompting?
6. Did participants repeatedly report existing communication as sufficient?
7. Did recipient experiences differ materially from sender experiences?
8. Did wearable or maintenance burden appear immediately problematic?
9. Did unexpected privacy or safety themes emerge?
10. Did sensitive-topic handling remain appropriate?
11. Was concept contamination avoided?
12. Does the wider protocol require amendment?
13. Should expenditure pause while the assumptions or method are reconsidered?

Do not create numerical pass thresholds from five qualitative interviews. Review
evidence strength, spontaneity, recency, behavioural specificity, repetition,
contradictions, participant variation and recruitment bias.
