## 1. Why This Research Is Being Conducted

Soul's Echo is exploring whether people experience meaningful unmet needs when
they are physically separated from someone important to them, and whether a
wearable product could provide a useful, respectful and private form of
connection.

The proposed Soul's Echo Band is only a concept. No final product, technology,
price, battery performance or release date has been approved.

This research may support a decision to proceed, proceed with changes, pivot,
stop or gather more evidence. Negative feedback and reasons not to develop the
product are as valuable as positive feedback.

## 2. What Participation May Involve

Participation may involve an individual interview or concept discussion lasting

Topics may include physical separation, communication habits, wearables,

Sensitive interpersonal situations may be discussed through hypothetical
scenarios. You will not be asked to use a product or prototype during this stage.

The first five interviews, if this session is part of that group, form an
**Early Diagnostic Review**. That review checks the interview method,
recruitment and early negative evidence. Five interviews cannot validate the
problem, product, market or Gate 1, and positive responses cannot justify project
progression.

## 3. Voluntary Participation

Participation is voluntary. You may decline any question, ask for clarification,
change topic, take a break or stop at any time without giving a reason.

No employer, partner, family member, university or organisation should require
you to participate. Payment already reasonably earned for your time must not
depend on positive feedback, answering every question or continuing after you
choose to stop.

## 4. Sensitive Topics and Limits

Hypothetical scenarios may concern unwanted contact, repeated interaction,
relationship deterioration, former partners, pressure to reconnect, lost devices
or privacy and safety controls.

Do not disclose information you are uncomfortable sharing. The researcher should
not probe traumatic personal experiences unnecessarily.

This research is not counselling, legal advice, medical advice, relationship
advice, crisis intervention or emergency support. Seek an appropriate qualified
or emergency service if you need that assistance.

## 5. Recording

The researcher may ask separately for affirmative permission to record the
session. Recording is not included automatically in participation consent and is
not required where the approved research method can reasonably proceed using
notes.

Before any recording, you must be told its format, purpose, storage, access,
transcription method and deletion rule. No automated transcription or external
recording tool may receive participant information until separately reviewed.

## 6. Notes and Data Categories

The researcher may take notes during or shortly after the session.

Where practical:

- contact details will be kept separately from research notes;
- notes will use a non-semantic participant identifier;
- unnecessary identifying details will not be recorded;
- de-identified notes will become the primary research record; and
- findings will be reported in aggregate.

Identifiable data directly identifies you. Pseudonymised data uses an identifier
but can still be connected to you through a separate mapping. De-identified data
has direct and unnecessary contextual identifiers removed. Aggregated findings
combine information from multiple participants.

Removing a name does not guarantee anonymity when context could still reasonably
identify someone.
